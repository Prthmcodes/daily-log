The protocol — read this when you're about to write the day off

  1. Never finish an episode. The compulsion isn't to start, it's to complete. So make stopping mid-sentence the normal thing. Pause at a random point and don't return. If you'd feel a pull to finish it, that's the exact thing to practise breaking.
  2. 90 seconds before play. You don't have to say no. You have to wait. Sit in the silence for 90 seconds, then decide. Most of the time the hand-reaching-for-play was never a decision at all, and 90 seconds is long enough for the wave to pass.
  3. Nothing in your ears while words are coming out of your head. Writing while listening to speech is two verbal streams fighting for one buffer. That isn't multitasking, it's interference. This rule is not negotiable, and it's the cheapest one to keep.
  4. The shower stays silent. Unstructured, low-stimulus time is where loose thoughts connect. It's the only slot in your day that does that job. Filling it costs you something you can't get back later.
  5. One real human contact per day. A voice in the room is a legitimate need, not a weakness. If you don't feed it deliberately, a stranger's podcast will. One call, one voice note, one conversation. It counts even if it's two minutes.
